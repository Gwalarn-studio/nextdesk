Remote Job Resume: Full Example + What to Change

A complete one-page example you can copy, and the six signals remote employers scan for in the first ten seconds.

Updated October 23, 2026
In short
  • A remote resume is a normal resume that proves you can work without someone watching.
  • Show results, written communication, the tools you use and your time zone.
  • Keep it to one page, one column, no photo, saved as PDF and Word.
  • Copy the example below and replace each line with your own numbers.

Remote employers receive applications from many countries for the same role. They read fast, and they look for one thing above all: evidence that you will deliver on your own. The good news is that you probably already have that evidence. You just need to put it where they look.

The six signals remote recruiters look for

  1. Results, not tasks. "Managed social media" says nothing. "Grew Instagram from 2,000 to 9,500 followers in 8 months" says you deliver.
  2. Written communication. Remote teams talk in writing. Mention documentation you wrote, reports, clients you handled by email.
  3. Autonomy. Projects you owned from start to finish, freelance work, anything you did without a manager next to you.
  4. Remote tools. Slack, Notion, Google Workspace, Zoom, Trello, Jira, Figma. List the ones you really use.
  5. Time zone and availability. One line in the header: "Based in Lyon, France (CET), available 9:00–17:00 CET".
  6. Past remote experience. If a job was remote or hybrid, write it next to the location: "Remote" or "Hybrid".
Tip: never had a remote job? Freelance projects, online courses with deadlines, and team work across offices all count as proof of autonomy.

Full example: customer support specialist (remote)

This example is for a mid-level profile applying to a remote customer support role. The structure works for almost any job: swap the details, keep the order.

Customer Support Specialist · Lyon, France (CET) · camille.martin@email.com · linkedin.com/in/camillemartin

Customer support specialist with 4 years in SaaS, 2 of them fully remote. I solve tickets fast, write help articles customers actually read, and work comfortably across time zones in English and French.

Section by section: what to write

Header

Name, target job title, city and country with time zone, email, LinkedIn. No photo, no date of birth, no full address: many international employers prefer you leave them out to avoid bias.

Summary (3 lines)

Who you are, your strongest result, and why you work well remotely. Write it last, once the rest is done.

Experience

Most recent first. Three bullets per job is enough. Each bullet starts with a verb and ends with a result: a number, a time saved, a problem solved. If you have no exact number, an honest estimate ("about a third") is better than nothing.

Skills

One line of tools and skills taken from the job ad. Recruiters and applicant tracking systems both scan this line. See our list of ATS keywords by job to pick the right words.

Education

One line per diploma. If you have more than 5 years of experience, it goes at the bottom.

Adapt it to each job in 10 minutes

  1. Copy the job ad into a document and highlight the skills and tools it repeats.
  2. Put the job title from the ad at the top of your resume, if it is honest.
  3. Rewrite your summary so its first line answers the ad's main need.
  4. Move your most relevant bullet to the top of each job.
  5. Add the missing tools you really know to your skills line.

You can ask an AI assistant to help with step 1. Paste the ad and ask: "List the 10 skills and tools this job ad asks for, most important first." Then check every line yourself: the AI does not know what you have really done.
