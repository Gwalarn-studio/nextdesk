11 ChatGPT Prompts to Review and Improve Your Resume

Copy-paste prompts that turn an AI chatbot into a strict resume reviewer, plus the privacy steps and checks that keep the result honest.

Updated October 23, 2026
In short
  • The 11 prompts below work in ChatGPT, Claude or Gemini. Copy one, fill the [brackets], paste your resume.
  • Remove your phone number, home address and email before you paste anything.
  • AI is good at structure, wording and spotting gaps. It is bad at facts about you: it can invent numbers and achievements.
  • Use one prompt at a time, then read every line before it goes into your resume.

Why use AI to review your resume?

A resume review needs a fresh pair of eyes. Friends are kind. Recruiters are busy. An AI chatbot is neither, so it can be a useful first reviewer. It reads fast, it never gets tired of a fifth version, and it is good at three things:

  • Comparing your resume with a job ad, line by line.
  • Rewriting long or vague sentences into short, active bullet points.
  • Asking questions you forgot to ask yourself, like "how many clients?" or "what changed after your project?"

It is not a recruiter, and it does not know your real career. Think of it as a sharp editor who has never met you. The prompts below are written to keep it in that role.

Before you paste: protect your personal data

A resume is full of personal data. Before you paste it into any chatbot, make a copy and clean it:

  • Delete your phone number, home address and personal email. The AI does not need them to review your wording.
  • Remove your date of birth, ID numbers, photo links and anything about health or family.
  • If you prefer, replace your name with "Candidate" and your current employer with "Company A".
  • Never paste a reference's contact details, or a client's confidential figures.

Also check your settings. As of October 2026, ChatGPT lets you turn off "Improve the model for everyone" under Settings > Data controls, and offers a Temporary Chat mode that is not used to train models (OpenAI says these chats may be kept for up to 30 days for safety). Details are on OpenAI's data controls page. Claude and Gemini have their own privacy settings, so look at them before you start.

Tip: keep a "clean" version of your resume in a separate file, with no contact details. Use only that file with AI tools. Add your contact block back at the very end.

How to use these prompts

  1. Click Copy on a prompt.
  2. Paste it into a new chat.
  3. Replace every [bracket] with your own text: the job ad, your resume, your target role.
  4. Read the answer. Ask follow-up questions. Keep only what is true.

One prompt per chat works best. If you paste several tasks at once, the answers get shorter and vaguer.

The 11 prompts

1. Review your resume against a job ad

This is the most useful prompt. Start here for every serious application.

Resume vs job ad review

You are an experienced recruiter for [type of role]. Compare my resume with the job ad below.

1. Give a match score from 1 to 10 and explain it in 3 lines.

2. List the 5 most important requirements in the ad and say if my resume shows each one: clearly, partly or not at all.

3. List the 5 weakest lines in my resume and explain why.

4. Suggest the 3 changes that would have the biggest impact.

Be direct. Do not invent experience I do not have. Job ad: [paste]. My resume: [paste].

2. Quantify your achievements

Numbers make a bullet point believable. But the AI cannot know your numbers, so this prompt makes it ask you instead of guessing.

Find the missing numbers

Here are bullet points from my resume. For each one, ask me 1 or 2 short questions that would help me add a real number (volume, time saved, money, people, frequency, percentage).

Do not write any numbers yourself. Wait for my answers, then rewrite each bullet using only the figures I give you.

Bullet points: [paste].

3. Rewrite weak bullet points

Stronger bullet points

Rewrite the bullet points below so each one starts with a strong action verb, shows a result, and fits on one or two lines.

Give 2 versions per bullet: one simple, one more detailed. Keep the same facts. If a bullet has no clear result, say "result missing" instead of inventing one.

Bullet points: [paste].

4. Shorten your resume to one or two pages

Cut to [1 or 2] pages

My resume is too long. I am applying for [target role]. Help me cut it to [1 / 2] pages.

1. List what to delete completely, and why.

2. List what to merge or shorten.

3. Show the shortened version of my experience section.

Keep everything relevant to the target role. Do not change dates, titles or facts. My resume: [paste].

5. Tailor your summary to a job

Tailored profile summary

Write 3 versions of a resume summary (3 to 4 lines each) for this job ad. Use only facts from my resume. Mention my years of experience, my main skills that match the ad, and one concrete result.

Avoid clichés like "passionate", "dynamic" or "results-driven". No first person ("I"). Job ad: [paste]. My resume: [paste].

6. Find missing ATS keywords

Many companies use an applicant tracking system (ATS) that lets recruiters search resumes by keyword. Our guide to ATS resume keywords explains how it works. This prompt does the comparison for you.

ATS keyword gap check

Extract the 15 most important keywords from this job ad (skills, tools, certifications, job titles). Keep the exact wording of the ad.

Make a table with: keyword / in my resume? (yes, different wording, no) / where I could add it honestly.

If my resume gives no evidence for a keyword, write "no evidence - do not add". Job ad: [paste]. My resume: [paste].

7. Translate your resume (and adapt it)

A resume is not just translated. Formats change between countries: photo or no photo, page length, how you write dates and degrees.

Translate and localize

Translate my resume from [source language] into [target language] for a job in [country].

Use natural, professional wording that a local recruiter would use, not a word-for-word translation. Keep job titles in their usual local form, and add the original in brackets if there is no clear equivalent.

After the translation, list any format habits in [country] I should know about (length, photo, personal details, dates). My resume: [paste].

8. Predict interview questions from your resume

Interview questions from my resume

You are the hiring manager for this job. Based on my resume and the job ad, list 10 questions you would ask me in an interview: 4 about my experience, 3 about gaps or weak points, 3 behavioral questions.

For each question, explain in one line what you are really trying to find out. Then ask me the first question and give feedback on my answer. Job ad: [paste]. My resume: [paste].

9. Explain a career gap or a career change

Gap or change, explained simply

I have [a gap of X months / a career change from A to B] because [short honest reason]. Suggest how to show this on my resume in one line, and give me a 30-second spoken answer for interviews.

Keep it honest, calm and short. Focus on what I did and learned, and on why I fit the new role. Do not invent activities.

10. Proofread like a strict editor

Final proofread

Proofread my resume. Check spelling, grammar, punctuation, verb tenses (past for old jobs, present for the current one), date formats and consistency (same style for every bullet and every date).

Show each problem as: original / correction / reason. Do not rewrite anything else. My resume: [paste].

11. Read it like a 30-second recruiter

The quick-skim test

Imagine you are a recruiter with 30 seconds to read my resume for a [target role] job.

Tell me: what job do you think I am applying for? What are the 3 things you remember? What made you hesitate? What would you want to see in the top third of the page?

My resume: [paste].

The limits: what AI gets wrong

AI chatbots write with confidence, even when they are wrong. On a resume, that is a real risk. Watch for these problems:

  • Invented achievements. Ask for "a stronger bullet" and you may get "increased sales by 35%" when you never measured sales. If you cannot explain a line in an interview, delete it.
  • Inflated job titles and skills. "Helped with reporting" can become "led data strategy". Recruiters may check, and reference calls can expose it.
  • Generic language. AI loves words like "leveraged", "spearheaded" and "dynamic". This style is easy to recognize, and simple words read better.
  • Wrong local rules. Advice on photos, length or personal details may not fit your country. Check with a local source.
  • Lost details. When shortening, AI sometimes drops dates, places or a key tool. Compare old and new versions side by side.

The rule is simple: AI suggests, you decide. Every number, title and claim in your final resume must be true and something you can talk about.

A simple 30-minute workflow

  1. Clean your resume: remove contact details and private data (5 min).
  2. Review against the job ad with prompt 1 (5 min).
  3. Fix the weak lines with prompts 2 and 3, using your own real figures (10 min).
  4. Tailor the summary and keywords with prompts 5 and 6 (5 min).
  5. Proofread with prompt 10, then read the whole thing yourself, out loud (5 min).
