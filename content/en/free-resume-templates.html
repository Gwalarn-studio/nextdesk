In short
  • You don't need a designer template. A plain, one-column resume is easier to read and safer with applicant tracking systems.
  • Below are three complete templates: classic, career change, and student or first job.
  • Copy one, paste it into Word or Google Docs, replace every line, then format it in five minutes.
  • Send it as a PDF unless the job ad asks for Word.

Many "free" resume templates online are not really free, or they come with two columns, icons and skill bars that look nice but confuse the software that reads your resume. The templates on this page are different. They are plain text. You copy them, paste them into the editor you already have, and add simple formatting yourself.

That way you control everything, the file stays light, and nothing breaks when a recruiter opens it on another computer.

How to use these templates

  1. Click Copy on the template you want.
  2. Open a new blank document in Word or Google Docs and paste.
  3. Replace every line with your own information. Delete anything that doesn't apply.
  4. Follow the formatting steps further down the page.
  5. Read it out loud once. Typos jump out when you hear them.
Tip: if you paste and the text looks odd, use "paste without formatting" (Ctrl+Shift+V on Windows, Cmd+Shift+V on Mac in Google Docs; in Word, use Paste > Keep Text Only). You will add your own styles afterwards.

Template 1: classic one-column resume

This is the format that works for most people, in most countries. One column, clear headings, results in each bullet. Recruiters scan it from top to bottom in a few seconds, so the most important information goes first.

First Name Last Name

Job Title You Want · City, Country (time zone if remote) · email@example.com · +00 0 00 00 00 00 · linkedin.com/in/yourname

Summary

[Job title] with [X] years of experience in [industry or type of work]. Known for [your main strength, e.g. solving problems fast, organizing complex projects, building trust with clients]. Looking to [what you want to bring to the next employer].

Experience

Job Title, Company Name · City or Remote · Month Year – Present

  • Action verb + what you did + result with a number (e.g. "Reduced invoice errors by 30% by creating a checklist used by the whole team").
  • Action verb + responsibility + scale (e.g. "Managed a portfolio of 40 client accounts").
  • One achievement you are proud of, with its impact.

Job Title, Company Name · City · Month Year – Month Year

  • Result-focused bullet.
  • Result-focused bullet.

Job Title, Company Name · City · Month Year – Month Year

  • One or two bullets are enough for older jobs.
Skills

Skill 1 · Skill 2 · Skill 3 · Software 1 · Software 2 · Software 3

Languages

English (native or level) · French (level, e.g. B2) · Other language (level)

Education

What to change first

  • The job title at the top. Use the title from the job ad if it honestly matches what you do.
  • The summary. Three lines maximum. The first line should answer the main need in the ad.
  • The bullets. Start each one with a verb (led, built, reduced, trained) and add a number where you can: volume, percentage, time saved, team size.

Template 2: career change resume

When you change fields, your job titles don't tell the right story. A recruiter in marketing won't connect "Restaurant Manager" to their needs on their own. This template helps them by putting transferable skills and relevant projects before your work history.

First Name Last Name

Target Job Title · City, Country · email@example.com · +00 0 00 00 00 00 · linkedin.com/in/yourname · portfolio link (optional)

Summary

[Previous profession] with [X] years of experience, now moving into [new field]. I bring [2–3 strengths that matter in the new job, e.g. client communication, budget management, team coordination] and recently completed [course, certificate or project] in [new field]. Looking for a [target role] where I can [specific contribution].

Transferable skills
  • Skill 1 (e.g. Project coordination): one sentence of proof from your past job, with a number.
  • Skill 2 (e.g. Customer relations): one sentence of proof.
  • Skill 3 (e.g. Data and reporting): one sentence of proof.
Relevant projects and training

Project name · Personal, volunteer or freelance project · Year

  • What you built or did, the tools you used, and the result.

Course or certificate name, Organization · Year

  • Main skills learned, or the final project you completed.
Professional experience

Job Title, Company Name · City · Month Year – Present

  • Pick the achievements that connect to your new field.
  • Translate jargon into plain words a new industry will understand.

Job Title, Company Name · City · Month Year – Month Year

  • One or two bullets, chosen for relevance.
Tools

Tool 1 · Tool 2 · Tool 3 · Tool 4

Languages

Language (level) · Language (level)

Education

Degree or Diploma, School Name, Year

Make the change believable

  • Show one concrete step. A course, a side project, volunteer work, or a freelance job in the new field. One real example beats a long list of intentions.
  • Rewrite old bullets for the new reader. "Handled 120 covers a night" becomes "Coordinated a team of 8 under time pressure, every evening".
  • Keep the dates honest. Don't hide your past. Explain it in the summary and in your cover letter.

Template 3: student or first job resume

Without much work experience, your education and projects carry the page. Put them first. Part-time jobs, internships and volunteering all count: they show you are reliable and can work with others.

Student in [field] · Looking for [internship / first job / apprenticeship] in [area] · City, Country · email@example.com · +00 0 00 00 00 00 · linkedin.com/in/yourname

Profile

[Year and field of study] student at [school], interested in [area]. Comfortable with [2–3 skills or tools]. Available from [date] for [type of contract and duration].

Degree name, School or University, City · Year – Year (expected)

Common mistakes on a first resume

  • Listing every course you have taken. Keep the three most relevant to the job.
  • Writing "motivated and dynamic". Show it with a project or a job instead.
  • Using a fun email address. Create a simple firstname.lastname address for applications.

Format your resume in Word

Once your text is in place, these settings take about five minutes.

  1. Font: select all (Ctrl+A or Cmd+A) and choose one simple font, such as Calibri, Arial or Aptos. Use 10.5 to 11 pt for the body text.
  2. Name: 18 to 20 pt, bold. Section headings: 12 pt, bold or small caps.
  3. Margins: go to Layout > Margins. "Normal" (2.54 cm / 1 inch) is fine. If you need space, choose "Narrow", but don't go under about 1.5 cm.
  4. Spacing: in the Paragraph settings, set line spacing to 1.0 or 1.15 and add a little space before each section heading.
  5. Bullets: select the bullet lines and click the bullet list button on the Home tab.
  6. Export to PDF: go to File > Save As (or File > Export) and choose PDF as the file type.

Format your resume in Google Docs

  1. Font: select all and pick a clear font such as Arial, Roboto or Lato, at 10.5 to 11 pt.
  2. Headings: make your name bold and larger, then make section titles bold. You can also apply "Heading 2" to them from the style menu.
  3. Margins: go to File > Page setup. Set the margins (for example 2 cm or 0.75 inch on each side) and choose A4 or Letter depending on your country.
  4. Spacing: use Format > Line & paragraph spacing to set "Single" and add space before headings.
  5. Export to PDF: go to File > Download > PDF Document (.pdf).
Tip: name your file clearly, for example FirstName-LastName-Resume.pdf. "CV_final_v3.pdf" doesn't help the recruiter find you later.

Before you send it: a quick check

  • One page if you have under about ten years of experience. Two pages maximum otherwise.
  • No tables, text boxes or columns for the main content. Some application software reads them badly.
  • No photo, unless it is the norm in the country you are applying to.
  • The same dates and job titles as on your LinkedIn profile. Our LinkedIn profile checklist helps you align both.
  • Open the PDF on your phone. If it reads well there, it will read well anywhere.
